1. Understanding agriculture in Enugu
Agriculture in Enugu State involves farming, livestock, fisheries, agro-processing, land access, mechanisation, inputs, extension services and market access. The Enugu State Government supports these areas through policy, funding, infrastructure and partnerships.

The state government does not control every farm, farmer or private company. Therefore, when a project involves private companies, federal institutions or development partners, AskEnugu should explain the role of the state government as a promoter, partner, regulator or supporter where that is what the available report establishes.

2. Agricultural situation before May 2023
Before Governor Peter Mbah assumed office on 29 May 2023, Enugu State already had a farming tradition. Many communities depended on crops, livestock, fisheries and local trade.

However, the state also faced challenges such as limited mechanisation, poor access to inputs, weak market links, insecurity in rural areas, poor road access and low value addition.

These problems did not begin with the Mbah administration. They formed part of the agricultural environment that the incoming government had to address.

3. The agricultural approach under Governor Peter Mbah
The administration’s approach has several connected parts. The first is expanding land access through a land bank. The second is developing farm estates across the state. The third is mechanisation through tractor assembly and farm equipment. The fourth is farmer support through inputs and registration. The fifth is agro-industrialisation through processing zones and value addition.

The idea behind this approach is that agriculture requires more than planting crops. Farmers need land, inputs, equipment, roads, storage, processing, markets and finance.

4. Major agricultural initiatives
4.1 Agricultural land bank
The administration has described a 300,000-hectare agricultural land bank. This is intended to make land more available for commercial farming and agro-industrial projects.

A land bank does not mean that all 300,000 hectares are already farmed or developed. It means the government has identified or secured land for agricultural use.

4.2 Smart Farm Estates
The administration has said it plans to establish 260 farm estates, with at least one in each electoral ward. Each estate is described as a 200-hectare farm estate with infrastructure such as warehouses, irrigation, tractor sheds, electricity, water supply and solar lighting.

The 2026 budget speech said the government intended to complete construction of all 260 farm estates in 2026. This is a plan or target, not confirmation that all 260 estates were completed by that date.

4.3 Tractor assembly and mechanisation
The state has partnered with Nortra Tractors to establish a tractor assembly plant. Reporting in 2026 said the partnership had assembled 107 tractors, intended to make mechanisation more accessible to farmers.

Mechanisation can support larger-scale farming, but its impact depends on affordability, maintenance, fuel or power supply, operator training and access to land. AskEnugu should not claim that every farmer now has access to a tractor.

4.4 Farmer support and inputs
In July 2026, the government launched distribution of farm inputs to 63,000 farmers. This is a specific, dated intervention and should be reported as such.

The state has also reported digital registration of more than 67,000 farmers. Farmer registration can help government plan inputs, extension services, credit and market support, but registration alone does not guarantee that every registered farmer received support.

4.5 Agro-industrial projects
The administration has also highlighted:

Expansion of Enugu United Palm Products Limited plantation to 6,917 hectares, with reported creation of more than 3,500 jobs.

Revival of Enugu Songhai Farms as a reported 10,000-hectare agro-industrial hub.

Plans for Special Agricultural Industrial Processing Zones, SAPZs, to support processing and value addition.

AskEnugu should distinguish between a plantation expansion, a processing zone plan, and actual production or job creation.
