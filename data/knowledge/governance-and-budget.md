1. Understanding governance and budgeting in Enugu
Governance in Enugu State involves the executive, legislature, judiciary, civil service, local governments and public institutions. The executive prepares budgets and implements policies. The legislature approves budgets and provides oversight. The judiciary interprets laws. Civil servants implement programmes. Local governments deliver services at the community level.

The state government does not control every institution. Therefore, AskEnugu should explain the role of each institution where relevant and avoid attributing every action to the governor personally.

2. Governance situation before May 2023
Before Governor Peter Mbah assumed office on 29 May 2023, Enugu State already had governance structures in place. These included the state executive council, the House of Assembly, the judiciary, ministries, departments, agencies and local governments.

However, the state also faced challenges such as limited transparency, weak revenue collection, infrastructure gaps, bureaucratic delays and dependence on federal allocations.

These problems did not begin with the Mbah administration. They formed part of the governance environment that the incoming government had to address.

3. The governance approach under Governor Peter Mbah
The administration’s approach has several connected parts. The first is budget expansion to support infrastructure and social services. The second is fiscal reform through revenue automation and financial management. The third is institutional reform through restructuring and capacity building. The fourth is transparency through digital systems and public reporting.

The idea behind this approach is that good governance requires more than good intentions. It requires clear budgets, strong institutions, reliable data, transparent processes and accountability.

4. Major governance initiatives
4.1 Budget growth
The administration has significantly increased the size of Enugu State’s budget. Reporting describes the budget rising from about ₦166 billion in 2023 to ₦521.5 billion in 2024, ₦971 billion in 2025, and about ₦1.62 trillion in 2026.

These are budget figures, not proof that every project was fully funded or completed. A budget shows planned spending; implementation depends on revenue, procurement, contractor performance and project management.

4.2 2026 budget priorities
The 2026 budget was presented as a “Budget of Renewed Momentum.” The governor said about ₦825.9 billion was allocated to the economic sector, with agriculture, industry and trade identified as priority areas for job creation, poverty reduction and revenue growth.

The 2026 budget also reportedly allocated:

About ₦49 billion for farm estates across the state.

About ₦27 billion for three Special Agricultural Industrial Processing Zones.

About ₦7 billion for tractors and farm implements.

About ₦2 billion for agro-inputs for the 2026 wet farming season.

These are budget allocations, not completed spending or completed projects.

4.3 Treasury Single Account
The Treasury Single Account, or TSA, is a financial-management system that brings government revenue and payments into a unified structure. It is intended to improve transparency, reduce leakages and make government spending more traceable.

The administration has described the TSA as part of its broader fiscal reforms. AskEnugu should explain it as a financial-management tool, not as a guarantee that all financial challenges have been solved.

4.4 Enterprise Resource Planning
Enterprise Resource Planning, or ERP, is a digital system that helps government manage data, finances, procurement, personnel and operations in one platform. It is intended to improve efficiency and reduce manual processes.

The administration has described ERP as part of its institutional reforms. AskEnugu should avoid claiming that every government process is now fully digital unless a specific system or module is documented.

4.5 Revenue automation
Revenue automation means using technology to collect, record and track government revenue. This includes e-payment systems, digital invoices, online portals and automated reporting.

The administration has described revenue automation as part of its effort to reduce leakages and improve IGR. AskEnugu should explain it as a reform in tax and revenue administration, not as a general claim that all government services are now digital.

4.6 Transparency and accountability
Governor Mbah has publicly emphasised inclusion, transparency and strong institutions as parts of governance. However, accountability also depends on timely publication of audited accounts, procurement records, project status and independent oversight.

AskEnugu should avoid presenting government claims as independent verification. Where a figure comes from the governor, ESIRS, or a state agency, it should be described as a government-reported figure.
