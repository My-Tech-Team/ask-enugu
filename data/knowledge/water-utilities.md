1. Understanding water and utilities in Enugu
Water supply in Enugu State is delivered through a combination of water schemes, pipelines, reservoirs, pumps, treatment facilities and distribution networks. The Enugu State Government supports these systems through funding, rehabilitation, construction, maintenance and policy.

The state government does not control every aspect of utility services. Electricity, for example, is largely managed through federal and private-sector structures. Therefore, AskEnugu should avoid claiming that the state government controls electricity generation or distribution unless a specific project or partnership is documented.

2. Water situation before May 2023
Before Governor Peter Mbah assumed office on 29 May 2023, Enugu State already faced water-supply challenges. These included ageing infrastructure, broken pipelines, low production capacity, poor distribution and limited household connections.

These problems did not begin with the Mbah administration. They formed part of the utility environment that the incoming government had to address.

The main challenges included restoring water production, replacing old pipes, improving transmission, expanding distribution and ensuring that water actually reaches homes and businesses.

3. The water approach under Governor Peter Mbah
The administration’s approach has several connected parts. The first is restoring production capacity through rehabilitation of existing water schemes. The second is improving transmission through new pipelines. The third is replacing old distribution pipes. The fourth is expanding household connections. The fifth is strengthening water institutions.

The idea behind this approach is that water supply requires more than building one facility. Water must be produced, treated, transmitted, distributed and maintained.

4. Major water initiatives
4.1 Ninth Mile Water Scheme
In November 2023, Governor Mbah commissioned the Ninth Mile 24/7 Water Scheme. The scheme was reported as capable of producing 70 million litres of water per day.

The scheme is intended to improve water production and support supply to Enugu metropolis. AskEnugu should describe it as a commissioned production facility, not as proof that every household now has 24/7 water.

4.2 Oji River water scheme
The administration has also reported work on the Oji River water scheme. The government said it planned to add new pumps at the scheme to deliver an additional 50 million litres per day.

This means the Oji River scheme is part of the wider plan to increase total water capacity. AskEnugu should distinguish between planned capacity and actual delivery.

4.3 Combined water target
The government targeted about 120 million litres per day, combining Ninth Mile capacity with planned additional capacity from the Oji scheme.

This is a target, not a confirmation that 120 million litres reaches every household every day. Actual supply depends on production, transmission, distribution networks, maintenance and household connections.

4.4 Ajali and Oji River waterworks
The administration has reported rehabilitation of the Ajali and Oji River waterworks. These projects are intended to improve water production and support supply to Enugu metropolis and surrounding areas.

Rehabilitation means repairing or upgrading existing facilities. It does not necessarily mean building a completely new water scheme.

4.5 Pipe replacement
The wider water programme, supported by the French Development Agency, reportedly includes replacement of about 240 kilometres of old asbestos pipes.

Asbestos pipes are old and can pose health and maintenance concerns. Replacing them with safer materials is intended to improve water quality, reduce leaks and strengthen the distribution network.

4.6 Nsude–Miliken Hill pipeline
In April 2026, the state government and the French Ambassador to Nigeria flagged off a 14.5-kilometre high-capacity transmission pipeline from Nsude to the Miliken Hill Terminal Reservoir. The project is intended to strengthen water transmission to Enugu metropolis.

This pipeline is important because transmission is different from distribution. Transmission moves water in bulk from a source to a major reservoir, while distribution moves water from the reservoir to homes and businesses.

4.7 Household connections
The wider water programme reportedly includes thousands of new water connections. This is intended to bring more households into the formal water-supply network.

AskEnugu should avoid claiming that every household in Enugu now has a connection. The programme is ongoing, and actual connections depend on infrastructure, funding and household-level implementation.

5. Utilities beyond water
Water is the most prominent utility reform in the available reporting. Other utility-related issues, such as electricity, sanitation, waste management and public lighting, may also affect daily life.

AskEnugu should only make specific claims about these areas where a project or policy is clearly documented. For example, a claim about electricity supply should identify the project, the partner, the location and the status.
