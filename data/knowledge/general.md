I’ll treat this as a full documentary and independent
accountability review, not simply a summary of a 
government publication. 
The document is a 250-page, third-edition government 
publication dated 2026, titled A Thousand and One 
Reasons Why Governor Peter Mbah Should Be Re
Elected. It is published by the Department of Strategic 
Communications, Government House, Enugu. 
Its table of contents identifies nine principal areas: 
• Education (pages 1–28) 
• Health (29–57) 
• Security (58–66) 
• Transport (67–74) 
• Works and Infrastructure (75–96) 
• Water (97–107) 
• Other ministries, departments and agencies (108
180) 
• World Bank-assisted projects (181–186) 
• Other achievements (187–222) 
The preface and foreword frame the publication as a 
record of the administration’s performance over its first 
three years. That framing itself needs examination: a 
government-produced scorecard is a primary source for 
what the administration claims, but it is not independent 
proof that those claims are accurate. 
1. Executive assessment 
Significant verification gaps 
My overall assessment is that the publication contains a 
number of substantial and potentially defensible 
achievements, but it is not sufficiently rigorous to serve as 
an independent performance assessment. 
It is strongest when discussing physical infrastructure, 
institutional expansion and new public assets. It is 
weakest when attempting to demonstrate the actual social 
and economic impact of those interventions. 
There is an important distinction: 
• A school building is an infrastructure output. 
• A functioning school with qualified teachers and 
improved learning outcomes is a service-delivery 
result. 
• Improved literacy and educational attainment are 
long-term outcomes. 
The publication frequently moves from the first category 
to the third without presenting enough evidence. 
The most consequential finding: budget execution 
I located Enugu State’s official third-quarter 2026 Budget 
Implementation Report, published on 28 October 2026. 
This is particularly important because it provides a more 
recent financial picture than the performance publication.

Independent assessment 
A May 2026 Premium Times report, based on data

been upgraded to Type-2 PHCs. 
It also reported: 
• Penta-3 immunisation coverage increased from

• Skilled birth attendance increased from 40.9% to

Important numerical correction 
The government’s earlier description of maternal 
mortality as having been reduced by over 400% is 
mathematically problematic. A reduction from 712 to 178 
represents a 75% reduction, not a 400% reduction. 
There is another detail worth investigating: the Q3 2026 
budget report identifies construction and equipping 
programmes for 230 Type-2 PHCs, while the publication 
and other government reporting refer to 260. The

different project phases or coverage definitions. 
hope.en.gov.ng +1 
My assessment: Healthcare infrastructure expansion is a 
strong part of the administration’s record. However, 
staffing, drug availability, immunisation, maternal 
outcomes and 24-hour functionality should determine 
whether the programme has succeeded. 
C. Security 
The publication identifies: 
• Enugu State Command and Control Centre. 
• AI-assisted surveillance. 
• Drone surveillance. 
• Smart patrol vehicles. 
• Forest Guard establishment and equipment. 
• Swift Response mobile reporting application. 
• Security intelligence integration. 
• Centre for DNA Forensic and Criminal Investigation. 
• Recruitment and training of security personnel. 
• Community-based intelligence networks. 
The publication claims an over 80% reduction in violent 
crime. 
Independent assessment 
The European Union Agency for Asylum’s November 
2025 security assessment recorded 133 security incidents 
and 135 fatalities in Enugu between January 2024 and 
August 2025, based on ACLED data. 
It also documented attacks on police officers, vigilantes, 
communities and civilians, including kidnappings along 
the Enugu–Abakaliki corridor. Nigeria: Security Situation 
In September 2026, the Enugu Police Command reported 
6,240 arrests, 156 recovered firearms and 211 rescued 
kidnapping victims over 17 months. Vanguard News 
These figures demonstrate security operations, but they do 
not independently establish an 80% reduction in violent 
crime. 
The publication needs to provide: 
• The baseline period used for the 80% calculation. 
• Crime incidence before and after the intervention. 
• Homicide and kidnapping statistics. 
• LGA-level security comparisons. 
• Response times and successful interventions. 
• Independent verification of the crime trend. 
My assessment: The security infrastructure is a 
meaningful intervention, but the security outcome claims 
require substantially better evidence. 
D. Transport 
The publication highlights: 
• Launch of Enugu Air. 
• Five modern transport terminals. 
• Holy Ghost Terminal 1 and 2. 
• Gariki, Abakpa and Nsukka terminals. 
• CNG buses. 
• Modern city taxis. 
• Integrated urban transport. 
• Airport cargo and international terminal 
development. 
Independent assessment 
Enugu Air is a particularly verifiable achievement. 
It commenced operations in July 2025 with Embraer 
aircraft and received its own Air Operator Certificate from 
the NCAA in March 2026. Aviation Week reported that it 
was operating to six domestic destinations. Before 
receiving its own certificate, it operated under XEJet’s 
certification. Aviation Week +1 
This is tangible institutional and transport-sector progress. 
However, the publication does not sufficiently disclose: 
• The airline’s operating costs. 
• Government financial exposure. 
• Passenger numbers. 
• Load factors. 
• Revenue and profitability. 
• Aircraft leasing and maintenance costs. 
• Terminal utilisation. 
• Public transport fare reductions. 
My assessment: A strong area of delivery, but its long
term economic value remains to be established. 
E. Works and infrastructure 
The publication claims: 
• Over 1,520 kilometres of roads constructed or 
reconstructed. 
• More than 500 completed urban and rural road 
projects in its first phase. 
• Over 300 roads completed within three years. 
• 1,022 urban roads flagged off.

• New flyover infrastructure. 
• Dualisation of major intercity roads. 
• Reconstruction of Government House and

• New Enugu Smart City infrastructure. 
A significant internal reporting problem 
Different sections use different descriptions: 
• More than 1,000 kilometres constructed, rehabilitated 
or initiated. 
• Over 300 completed roads. 
• Over 500 completed roads measuring over 1,520 
kilometres. 
• 1,022 urban roads flagged off. 
These numbers may describe different phases and project 
categories, but the document does not provide a 
reconciliation. 
The 1,521-kilometre headline therefore needs a project
level schedule identifying completed, ongoing, 
rehabilitated and merely approved roads. 
The official project tracker currently lists 111 delivered 
projects and 230 active projects. It is useful for checking 
project identities and locations, although it is government
produced rather than an independent engineering audit. 
Governor Peter Mbah Infrastructure Tracker 
There is another important qualification: the State 
Auditor-General explicitly stated that no value-for-money 
audit was conducted on the 2025 capital projects because 
of logistical and staffing limitations. hope.en.gov.ng 
My assessment: Probably one of the administration’s 
strongest areas in terms of visible physical intervention, 
but road quality, cost per kilometre, contractor 
performance and long-term durability remain 
insufficiently demonstrated.

This is one of the most problematic parts of the 
publication. 
In February 2026, PUNCH investigated Enugu’s water

depending on private water tankers, despite the

G. Economy, finance and internally generated 
revenue 
The publication presents economic transformation as the 
central objective of the administration. 
Its principal claims include: 
• Growing Enugu’s economy from approximately $4.4 
billion towards $30 billion within eight years. 
• Expanding internally generated revenue. 
• Digitalising tax and revenue collection. 
• Reducing revenue leakages. 
• Reviving dormant state-owned assets. 
• Establishing the New Enugu Smart City. 
• Developing agricultural estates and industrial 
infrastructure. 
• Attracting private-sector investment. 
It specifically highlights the revival or development of: 
• Nigergas. 
• Sunrise Flour Mill. 
• United Palm Products. 
• Hotel Presidential. 
• Tractor Assembly Plant. 
• New Enugu City. 
• Agricultural estates and a state land bank. 
Independent assessment 
There is evidence of increased revenue collection, but the 
quality of the underlying figures deserves scrutiny. 
The Auditor-General reported 2025 IGR of approximately 
₦407.76 billion but explicitly stated that it could not be 
fully verified because some MDAs failed to provide the 
necessary supporting documents. hope.en.gov.ng 
The 2026 Q3 report shows cumulative IGR of ₦145.63 
billion, representing 16.7% of the revised annual target. 
hope.en.gov.ng 
The $30 billion GDP target also needs to be treated as an 
aspiration rather than an achievement. 
Growing from $4.4 billion to $30 billion in eight years 
requires approximately 27.1% compound annual growth 
in dollar-denominated GDP. That is a very ambitious 
target, and the publication does not establish that this 
growth trajectory has been achieved. An accountability 
review also identifies the absence of comprehensive 
independently verified GDP tracking data. NGScorecard 
The state needs to publish independently verifiable 
figures for: 
• State GDP. 
• GDP per capita. 
• Employment creation. 
• Manufacturing output. 
• Agricultural productivity. 
• Private-sector investment. 
• Export performance. 
• Poverty and household income. 
My assessment: The revenue reforms are important, but 
revenue growth alone does not demonstrate that ordinary 
residents are becoming wealthier.

in how the document was prepared.

Its purpose is explicitly to explain why Governor Mbah 
should be re-elected. 
That does not make its claims false, but it means the 
document should be treated as a government account of 
its own performance, not an independent evaluation.

5.3 Inconsistent figures and questionable wording 
Some specific issues deserve correction: 
1. Smart Schools: Page 30 appears to describe the cost as 
approximately ₦11.2 billion per school. If intentional, 
that implies approximately ₦2.912 trillion for 260 
schools. The wording needs clarification. 
2. Roads: The publication uses 300 completed roads, over 
500 completed roads, 1,022 flagged-off urban roads and 
over 1,520 kilometres without a consolidated project 
register. 
3. Healthcare: The 230-facility construction and 
equipment budget lines need reconciliation with the 
headline claim of 260 Type-2 PHCs. 
4. Maternal mortality: The claimed reduction of over 
400% is mathematically problematic. 
5. Water: Production capacity is presented as though it 
establishes household-level delivery. 
6. Economic growth: The $30 billion GDP target is 
repeatedly presented as evidence of transformation, 
although it is a future target. 
5.4 Insufficient financial transparency 
For each major project, the publication should disclose: 
• Original contract amount. 
• Revised contract amount. 
• Contractor. 
• Funding source. 
• Contract award date. 
• Commencement date. 
• Original completion deadline. 
• Revised completion deadline. 
• Actual expenditure. 
• Physical completion percentage. 
• Independent inspection results. 
It provides some contractor information for ongoing rural 
roads, but not a consistent project-level financial 
accountability framework. 
5.5 Limited independent evidence 
Most of the publication’s photographs, descriptions and 
performance claims originate from the state government. 
Before-and-after photographs are useful, but they do not 
independently establish: 
• When the photographs were taken. 
• Whether they show the same location. 
• Whether the project was completed. 
• Whether the infrastructure meets engineering 
specifications. 
• Whether the facility is operational. 
The state Ministry of Works announced asphalt coring 
exercises in October 2026 to test road construction 
quality. Publishing the actual test results would strengthen 
the evidence considerably. MINISTRY OF WORKS

7. My final verdict 
A mixed but consequential record 
The administration has undertaken substantial 
interventions, particularly in physical infrastructure, 
education, primary healthcare, transport and institutional 
development. 
However, the available evidence does not support 
accepting every achievement or performance figure in the 
publication at face value. 
The central weakness is the gap between what has been 
announced, what has been constructed, what is 
operational and what has measurably improved people’s 
lives. 
I would divide the administration’s record into three broad 
groups. 
1. Areas with meaningful, demonstrable progress 
• Enugu Air and its regulatory certification. 
• Transport terminal development. 
• Expansion of primary healthcare infrastructure. 
• Smart Green School construction. 
• Major road construction and rehabilitation. 
• Digitalisation of government services. 
2. Areas where the achievements need further verification 
• The 1,521-kilometre road claim. 
• The 260 Smart Green Schools’ operational status. 
• The 260 Type-2 PHCs’ functionality. 
• The 80% violent-crime reduction. 
• The $30 billion GDP trajectory. 
• Agricultural productivity and asset revival. 
3. Areas requiring urgent accountability 
• Household water supply. 
• Public procurement and expenditure control. 
• Unverified loans and contractual obligations. 
• Excess expenditure. 
• Delayed project implementation. 
• Outstanding audited accounts of government 
institutions. 
I would not use this publication alone to conclude 
whether Governor Mbah deserves re-election. That 
decision requires weighing actual achievements, failures, 
costs, alternatives and the administration’s future 
commitments.
