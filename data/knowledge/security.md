This document is a detailed reference about security in Enugu State. It explains the security environment before Governor Peter Mbah assumed office on 29 May 2023, the security challenges that continued into his administration, and the policies, structures, equipment, deployments, operations and community initiatives associated with his government.

The purpose is to give AskEnugu AI enough connected information to answer natural questions. The answers should explain the subject in ordinary language and connect the facts rather than return isolated bullet points.

The account is written to make the administration's actions easy to understand and recognise. It does not claim that every security problem has disappeared. It distinguishes state government initiatives from Nigeria Police Force operations, military operations and programmes launched by local government councils. That distinction helps AskEnugu give accurate credit to the right institution.

Dates and numbers should be repeated with their time period and source context. 1. Understanding Enugu's security responsibilities

Security in Enugu State is delivered through several institutions working together. The Nigeria Police Force has the primary responsibility for policing, investigation, arrest and prosecution support. The Nigerian Army and other federal security agencies may conduct operations where required. The Department of State Services performs intelligence and internal security functions. The Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps has statutory security and protection responsibilities. The Enugu State Government supports the wider security system through funding, infrastructure, technology, logistics, coordination and community-based initiatives.

The state government does not command every federal security agency in the same way it commands its own ministries. Therefore, when a joint operation takes place, AskEnugu should explain the role of the state government as a mobiliser, supporter or coordinating partner where that is what the available report establishes. It should not automatically describe every police or military operation as an operation personally conducted by the governor.

Community-based security also has a long history in Enugu. Neighbourhood Watch groups existed before 2023. The Mbah administration's contribution described in the sources includes efforts to reform, revitalise, equip and coordinate these groups, as well as support specific local arrangements.

2. Enugu's security situation before May 2023

Before Governor Peter Mbah took office on 29 May 2023, Enugu State already faced several security pressures. These included attacks on communities, killings, kidnapping, attacks on security personnel, communal and farmer-herder tensions, criminal activity around rural and forested areas, and the wider South-East security crisis. These problems did not begin with the Mbah administration. They formed part of the security environment that the incoming government had to address.

2.1 Attacks on communities and rural settlements

Communities in parts of Uzo-Uwani and Isi-Uzo experienced serious violence before May 2023. In March 2021, deadly violence was reported in Adani, Uzo-Uwani, during clashes involving herders and local security actors. In November 2022, attacks were reported in communities in Isi-Uzo, where residents described killings and destruction. The January 2022 attack in Isi-Uzo and the reported destruction of homes and businesses showed how insecurity could affect not only lives but also farming, movement, livelihoods and the ability of residents to remain in their communities.

These incidents are important background because they show that rural insecurity, disputes around land and farming, and the use of forested areas by armed groups were already concerns before 2023. They also help explain why later interventions in Isi-Uzo, Uzo-Uwani and other rural LGAs placed emphasis on patrols, forest surveillance, access roads and community intelligence.

2.2 Kidnapping and attacks on individuals

Kidnapping and abduction were also part of the pre-2023 security picture. People travelling on roads, residents in rural communities and individuals returning from social or political activities faced security risks in different parts of the state. The wider South-East experienced a period of armed attacks and criminal activity that affected public confidence, movement and economic life.

2.3 Attacks on police and public institutions

Enugu also experienced attacks on public and security institutions before May 2023. In February 2022, an armed group attacked an Independent National Electoral Commission facility in Amagunze, Nkanu East, killing five people according to the 2023 Nigeria election observation report. In March 2023, two police officers were reported killed in an attack on a police checkpoint near the University of Nigeria Enugu Campus area. These incidents illustrate the security pressures facing public institutions and law enforcement personnel before the change of administration.

2.4 Sit-at-home enforcement and its effect on daily life

The wider South-East sit-at-home crisis affected movement, trading, transport and public activity. In Enugu, threats and violence associated with enforcement of sit-at-home orders contributed to public concern and disruption. The security challenge was not limited to conventional street crime. It also involved intimidation, attacks and the difficulty of ensuring that residents could move freely, open businesses and carry out normal activities.

2.5 Existing community security structures

Neighbourhood Watch and community vigilante arrangements were already present in Enugu before 2023. The state had a legal and administrative history of community security structures, and earlier governments had organised and trained neighbourhood watch members. Forest Guards also existed before the Mbah administration. This is important context: the later administration did not invent every community security structure from the beginning. Its documented contribution includes renewed attention, restructuring, expanded surveillance, training, logistics and coordination.

2.6 What the inherited situation meant for the new administration

The main challenges confronting the state included protecting rural settlements, improving response to distress calls, monitoring roads and public spaces, strengthening intelligence, securing forest corridors, protecting farms and border communities, improving coordination among security agencies, and restoring confidence that security incidents would receive a timely response. The Mbah administration made security a major part of its early governing agenda and built a combination of technology, rapid response, funding and community-level interventions around these needs.

3. The security approach under Governor Peter Mbah

The administration's approach has several connected parts. The first is surveillance and intelligence, using CCTV cameras, monitoring systems and a central Command and Control Centre. The second is rapid response, through the Distress Response Squad and operational support for security agencies. The third is rural and forest security, through Forest Guard operations and surveillance installations. The fourth is financing and equipment, including the Enugu State Security Trust Fund. The fifth is community participation, through Neighbourhood Watch groups, local government initiatives and engagement with residents.

The idea behind this approach is that security requires more than the presence of uniformed personnel. Authorities need information about what is happening, a way to communicate that information, personnel and vehicles that can respond, equipment to support operations, and cooperation from communities that understand local conditions.

4. Major security institutions and initiatives

4.1 Security Command and Control Centre

The Security Command and Control Centre is a central facility designed to bring surveillance, information and security coordination together. Rather than having information remain isolated in separate locations, the centre is intended to help security agencies monitor selected locations, receive information and coordinate responses.

In his first-anniversary address on 29 May 2024, Governor Mbah described the centre as a technology-enabled platform providing round-the-clock surveillance of major streets and neighbourhoods through CCTV cameras placed at strategic locations. In his 2025 budget presentation, he said the centre had been completed and equipped during 2024, and described a state CCTV network using AI-enabled cameras.

The administration has described the surveillance system as including fixed cameras and mobile cameras. The intended uses include monitoring public spaces and roads, identifying suspicious activity, tracking vehicles or movements relevant to an investigation, supporting intelligence gathering and helping response teams act on information. The centre is also described as a coordination point for security agencies, including the police, military and DSS.

For AskEnugu: if a user asks what the Command and Control Centre does, explain that it is the central monitoring and coordination facility in the state's security architecture. Do not claim that every street, village or LGA has identical camera coverage. The public descriptions establish a statewide system, but do not provide a complete public camera-by-camera map.

4.2 Distress Response Squad (DRS)

The Distress Response Squad is the rapid-response component associated with the state's security system. It was established early in the Mbah administration to improve the ability to respond when a security incident is reported. The concept is straightforward: information about an incident must be received, assessed and passed to personnel who can respond quickly.

The DRS works alongside the broader security system and is supported by patrol vehicles, communication equipment and the Command and Control Centre. In the governor's 2025 budget presentation, he said more than 100 patrol vehicles fitted with wide-angle AI-enabled street cameras had been procured to strengthen DRS operations. By December 2025, media reporting referred to a fleet of more than 150 vehicles fitted with AI-enabled cameras.

The administration also reported procuring more than 500 communication radios, helmets and body cameras for personnel in the 2024 security expansion. These items support communication, personnel protection and operational documentation. When explaining the DRS, AskEnugu should describe its purpose as rapid response and operational support, not as a replacement for the police or other statutory security agencies.

4.3 Enugu State Security Trust Fund (ESSTF)

The Enugu State Security Trust Fund is a financing and support mechanism intended to provide a more sustainable way to mobilise resources for security. It brings together government support and contributions from private-sector organisations, individuals and other stakeholders to strengthen security infrastructure, equipment, capacity building and technology.

The fund was inaugurated in February 2024 and an 11-member Board of Trustees was announced. Governor Mbah projected security spending of approximately ₦20 billion for 2024 and called for private-sector contributions. The ₦20 billion figure was a projected security expenditure figure, not a statement that the Trust Fund itself raised or spent that exact amount.

The Trust Fund's published strategic objectives include commissioning security assets, fundraising, capacity building, technological advancement, advisory support and community engagement. Its official website lists the donation and commissioning of two vertical take-off and landing surveillance drones, 10 Hilux vehicles, 40 motorcycles, 400 bulletproof vests and 400 helmets on 1 December 2025.

A key point for AskEnugu is that the Security Trust Fund's underlying legal framework predates the Mbah administration. The administration's contribution was to activate and strengthen its implementation and use it as a platform for funding and supporting security operations.

4.4 Forest Guards and forest surveillance

Forest security is important in Enugu because some communities are surrounded by forests, difficult terrain and rural routes that can be hard to monitor. Forested areas can be used for farming and other legitimate activities, but remote locations can also be exploited by criminal groups seeking concealment or escape routes.

The Mbah administration strengthened the Forest Guard structure through leadership appointments, training, surveillance and deployment. In June 2025, retired Colonel Dan Didiugwu was appointed chairman and retired Deputy Commissioner of Police Olamitisoji Akinbamilayo was appointed commander of the Forest Guard.

In May 2025, the state government identified 11 LGAs with forest-surveillance installations: Nsukka, Udi, Isi-Uzo, Aninri, Igbo-Eze South, Udenu, Awgu, Igbo-Eze North, Uzo-Uwani, Ezeagu and Igbo-Etiti. These installations form part of the wider monitoring arrangement and are intended to support detection and coordination with ground patrols.

Forest Guard activity is particularly relevant to rural communities, farming settlements, border areas and locations where roads and terrain make ordinary patrol coverage difficult. AskEnugu should explain that Forest Guards work within the wider state and federal security environment and should not describe them as a replacement for the police or military.

4.5 Police operations and specialist response

The Nigeria Police Force remains the lead institution for ordinary policing, criminal investigation and law enforcement. The Mbah administration has provided support to the security environment through infrastructure, logistics, technology, equipment and coordination. The Enugu Police Command has also publicised specialised operational structures, including the Violent Crime Response Unit (VCRU), introduced in 2026.

The VCRU is designed to focus on violent and organised crime, including armed robbery, kidnapping, unlawful possession of firearms, cultism and drug-related offences. A police statement reported in October 2026 said the unit had been established in April 2026. At its launch, the Commissioner of Police reported that the unit had arrested 182 suspects, rescued 31 kidnapping victims and recovered 57 firearms, including 13 AK-47 rifles, as well as 669 rounds of ammunition and 44 vehicles. These are police-reported operational figures for the VCRU since its establishment.

The unit's reported structure includes specialised sections for armed robbery, terrorism-related offences, kidnapping, cultism and drug trafficking, as well as operational monitoring, professional conduct oversight and media liaison. The police statement acknowledged the governor's support for vehicular logistics and security infrastructure.

4.6 Training and professional development

Technology and equipment are useful only when personnel know how to use them. In June 2026, the Federal Ministry of Information and National Orientation reported that the Enugu State Government and police organised a five-day training programme for hundreds of personnel drawn from the Nigeria Police Force, NSCDC, Enugu State Forest Guards and Neighbourhood Watch.

The programme, themed 'Building Smarter, Safer and Secure Communities', focused on data gathering and cybersecurity. The training included facilitators with experience in cybercrime and police training. This reflects an effort to strengthen the skills needed for technology-supported policing, information gathering and cooperation among different security groups.

4.7 Neighbourhood Watch and community security

Neighbourhood Watch groups provide a community-level link between residents and formal security agencies. Members are often familiar with local roads, households, farms, movement patterns and community concerns. When properly organised and coordinated, they can help report suspicious activity, share local intelligence and support preventive patrols.

In September 2023, the Mbah administration began a sensitisation tour across all 17 LGAs to reform and revitalise Neighbourhood Watch groups. The stated purpose was to improve their effectiveness and encourage closer cooperation with the police and other law enforcement agencies. The tour included visits to Uzo-Uwani and Ezeagu.

In January 2026, the state government reconstituted a 20-member Neighbourhood Watch in Nomeh-Unateze, Nkanu West. The initiative was intended to strengthen grassroots crime prevention, community intelligence and collaboration with the police. Reports referred to operational logistics and stipends for operatives, alongside an emphasis on discipline and human-rights standards.

The state also supports wider community policing engagement. In May 2026, the Commissioner of Police held a town hall at the Udenu Local Government Secretariat with stakeholders from Udenu, Igbo-Etiti, Igbo-Eze North and Isi-Uzo. The discussions addressed local security concerns, police conduct, intelligence sharing and cooperation between residents and the police.

4.8 Road access and security

Road infrastructure can contribute to security when it improves access to remote communities, enables patrol vehicles to reach settlements, supports emergency response and reduces the isolation of farming areas. This is particularly relevant in Isi-Uzo and other rural parts of Enugu.

The administration's reported interventions in Isi-Uzo combined security deployments with road projects intended to improve access to vulnerable communities. The Ogbete–Agu–Mgbuji road was reported at approximately 21.7 to 22 kilometres. Other road interventions were described as linking communities and supporting security operations around border and forest areas.

Road construction is not itself a police operation, but improved access can make it easier for residents, emergency services and security personnel to move between communities. AskEnugu should explain this connection without claiming that a road project alone eliminates insecurity.

6. Specific security interventions across the 17 LGAs

The following section records the most identifiable security-related activities reported for each local government area. Some activities were direct state government initiatives, some involved federal security agencies, and some were led by local government councils with the stated aim of complementing the wider state security effort.

Enugu East

In October 2024, security-related operations were reported at Nkwubor Layout in Emene, Ogbeke Nike and areas around Ugwuogo-Nike. The operations targeted properties identified by the government as suspected criminal or kidnapping hideouts. Reports said an uncompleted building with an underground tunnel contained arms and ammunition. The activities formed part of efforts to disrupt locations allegedly used by criminal groups. The government and media descriptions identify the properties as suspected hideouts; the account should not describe allegations as convictions.

Enugu North

Enugu North is a central urban local government area containing major commercial, public and residential locations. It benefits from the state's wider urban security architecture, including the Command and Control Centre, strategic CCTV surveillance and DRS patrol activity. ACLED data analysed by the European Union Agency for Asylum recorded 18 security incidents in Enugu North between January 2024 and August 2025, the second-highest LGA count in that dataset. The figure helps explain why urban monitoring, public-space safety and rapid response remain important in the area.

Enugu South

The administration's boundary-resolution process is relevant to communal and land-related disputes affecting Enugu South and neighbouring communities. A land dispute in Etiti-Ngwo was reported in May 2025 and involved destruction of property. The wider urban surveillance and DRS arrangements also form part of the security support available to the area. The boundary-resolution work is intended to prevent local disagreements from escalating into violence.

Isi-Uzo

Isi-Uzo, particularly Eha-Amufu and nearby farming communities, has been a major focus of security attention. Reports described a military camp at Ogbete, security buffer zones around affected border communities and the deployment of more than 200 military and security personnel across Eha-Ohuala, Aguamede and Ikem-Nkwo in June 2024. Road interventions, including the Ogbete–Agu–Mgbuji road project reported at approximately 21.7 to 22 kilometres, were intended to improve access to farming settlements and support security operations. Isi-Uzo was also included in the 11-LGA forest-surveillance programme. The scale of these activities reflects the security challenges around rural settlements, border communities and forest corridors.

Nkanu East

The Enugu State Boundary Committee's work addressed a longstanding land dispute involving communities in Nkanu East and neighbouring Nkanu West. The Amechi–Idodo–Amagun–Amagunze road project was also listed among infrastructure works in the area. Boundary dispute resolution is relevant to security because unresolved land disagreements can lead to tension, destruction of property and violence. Better road access can also support movement and emergency response.

Nkanu West

In January 2026, the state government reconstituted a 20-member Neighbourhood Watch in Nomeh-Unateze. The group was intended to improve grassroots crime prevention, local intelligence and cooperation with the police. Reports also referred to operational logistics and stipends for operatives, with emphasis on discipline and respect for human rights. The Nomeh-Unateze arrangement is one of the clearest examples of a specifically identified community security intervention in the LGA.

Aninri

Aninri was included in the state government's May 2025 list of 11 LGAs with forest-surveillance installations. The installations form part of the wider monitoring system intended to support the detection of suspicious movement and coordination with ground patrols in rural and forested locations. This connects Aninri to the state's broader rural and forest security programme.

Awgu

In October 2024, a security operation was reported at Amabor Owelli, where a property was identified by the government as a suspected kidnapping holding location. The action was reported as following an abduction and detention of a victim at the property. Awgu was also among the 11 LGAs named in the forest-surveillance programme. The two interventions relate to efforts to disrupt kidnapping operations and improve monitoring in rural terrain.

Ezeagu

Ezeagu was included in the May 2025 list of 11 LGAs with forest-surveillance installations. In September 2023, the state government's Neighbourhood Watch sensitisation and revitalisation tour also visited Ezeagu. The engagement focused on improving the performance of local watch groups and strengthening cooperation with police and other security agencies. These initiatives combine rural monitoring with community participation.

Oji River

In January 2025, the Oji River council chairman reported local security measures that included digital surveillance to help identify security dark spots, clearing access roads in Uzo-Agu, Elugwu Akwu and Agu Akwu, and removing roadside vegetation that could provide cover for ambushes. The report also referred to training for the local Chief Security Officer and deputies, support for Neighbourhood Watch and local security vehicles. These were reported as local government council measures, intended to complement the wider state security environment.

Udi

Udi was included in the 11-LGA forest-surveillance programme and was also named in 2024 reporting on security operations against suspected kidnapping groups operating from forest locations. ACLED data analysed by EUAA recorded 12 security incidents in Udi between January 2024 and August 2025. The area's forest surveillance and security operations are relevant to protecting communities and monitoring rural routes.

Uzo-Uwani

Uzo-Uwani has received several identifiable security interventions. On 10 May 2025, the Commissioner of Police led a patrol through Nimbo, Opanda, Adani, Ogurugu and Nkpologu. The LGA was also included in the forest-surveillance programme. In December 2025, the local council chairman launched Operation Kwechiri, a local security initiative reported to involve 320 trained vigilantes, one Hilux gun truck, three Sienna vans and 25 motorcycles, with a plan for 24-hour surveillance across the LGA's 16 political wards. Operation Kwechiri was launched by the local government council to complement the wider state security effort. It should not be described as a programme personally created by the governor.

7. Key security operations, rescues and police-reported results

7.1 Rescue of nine abducted people in Igbo-Eze South

On 26 April 2025, gunmen abducted nine people at Eburumiri, Ibagwa-Aka, in Igbo-Eze South. The victims included two Catholic reverend sisters. According to Premium Times reporting on the statement by the local council chairperson, the victims were rescued on 1 May during a joint security operation. The local council chairman said the Enugu State Government helped coordinate and mobilise security resources, while neighbouring communities in Nsukka and communities across the Kogi boundary assisted the operation. The rescued people were reported to have been reunited with their families and taken for medical evaluation and psychological support.

This operation is relevant because it shows how kidnapping response can depend on several groups working together. The police, military, local security personnel, Forest Guards, local authorities and neighbouring communities all had roles in the wider response.

7.2 Security operations in Enugu East and Awgu

In October 2024, operations were reported against properties identified as suspected kidnapping hideouts in Enugu East and Awgu. In Enugu East, locations mentioned included Nkwubor Layout in Emene, Ogbeke Nike and areas around Ugwuogo-Nike. In Awgu, a property at Amabor Owelli was identified as a suspected holding location. These operations were presented as efforts to disrupt infrastructure allegedly used by kidnapping groups.

7.3 Isi-Uzo security deployment

In Isi-Uzo, the reported military camp at Ogbete, buffer zones and deployment of more than 200 military and security personnel across Eha-Ohuala, Aguamede and Ikem-Nkwo were intended to strengthen protection around vulnerable communities, border locations and forest areas. Road interventions were linked to the same objective by improving access to rural settlements and routes used by security personnel.

7.4 Police operational figures reported in September 2026

On 7 September 2026, Premium Times reported figures provided by the Enugu State Commissioner of Police, Mamman Giwa, covering 17 months from his assumption of office on 11 March 2025. The police said the command detained 6,240 suspects, recovered 156 firearms and 1,486 rounds of assorted ammunition, and rescued 211 kidnapped victims during the period.

The police said the detained suspects included 158 kidnapping suspects, 186 armed robbery suspects, 188 murder suspects, 57 rape suspects, 273 cultism suspects and 63 suspects for unlawful possession of firearms. The remaining 5,315 were detained for other offences, including stealing, breaking in and vandalism. The police also reported recovering 43 AK-47 rifles and one G-3 assault rifle, along with 209 vehicles, tricycles and motorcycles.

The Commissioner of Police attributed the results to intelligence-led operations, proactive policing and cooperation with neighbouring states. He also referred to the VCRU, intensified border patrols under the Inspector-General of Police's Handshake Patrol initiative, and improved emergency response through the DRS and Command and Control Centre. These are police-reported operational figures, not a complete crime total for the state and not a count of convictions.

7.5 VCRU operational figures reported in October 2026

A police statement published on 15 October 2026 reported that the VCRU had arrested 182 suspects, rescued 31 kidnapping victims and recovered 57 firearms since the unit was established in April 2026. The recovered firearms included 13 AK-47 rifles. The unit also reported recovering 669 rounds of assorted live ammunition and 44 vehicles, including motor vehicles, tricycles and motorcycles. The statement said many of the suspects had been prosecuted.

These VCRU figures cover the unit's operations since its establishment. They are a subset of the wider police command's reported activity and should not be added to the 17-month police figures as if they were separate totals without checking for overlap.

9. How the security initiatives fit together

The Command and Control Centre, CCTV network, DRS, Forest Guards, police operations, Security Trust Fund and Neighbourhood Watch groups are parts of a connected security arrangement. Each has a different role.

Surveillance systems help authorities observe selected public spaces and identify information that may require attention.

The Command and Control Centre brings monitoring and coordination together.

The DRS supports rapid response when incidents are reported.

Police investigate offences, make arrests and work with prosecutors and courts.

The military and other federal security agencies provide support in operations requiring their capabilities.

Forest Guards support monitoring and security activity in rural and forested areas.

Neighbourhood Watch groups provide local knowledge and help residents communicate concerns to formal agencies.

The Security Trust Fund provides a structure for raising and directing resources towards equipment, technology and capacity building.

The value of the overall approach is in coordination. Surveillance without response is limited. Response without reliable information can be delayed. Equipment without trained personnel cannot deliver its full value. Community intelligence is most useful when residents can report concerns safely and the relevant authorities respond appropriately.

It is a local security initiative launched by the Uzo-Uwani Local Government Council in December 2025. Reports said it involved 320 trained vigilantes, one Hilux gun truck, three Sienna vans and 25 motorcycles, with a plan for 24-hour coverage across 16 political wards.

Were nine kidnapped people rescued in Igbo-Eze South?

Yes. Nine people, including two Catholic reverend sisters, abducted at Eburumiri, Ibagwa-Aka, on 26 April 2025 were reported rescued during a joint operation on 1 May 2025. The local council chairman credited coordination and resource mobilisation by the Enugu State Government and cooperation from neighbouring communities and multiple security formations.

What is the VCRU?

The Violent Crime Response Unit is a specialised unit of the Enugu State Police Command established in April 2026 to address violent and organised crime, including kidnapping, armed robbery, cultism and offences involving illegal firearms.

What were the VCRU's reported results?

A police statement in October 2026 reported 182 arrests, 31 kidnapping victims rescued, 57 firearms recovered, 669 rounds of ammunition and 44 vehicles recovered since the unit's establishment in April 2026.

What were the police command's reported results over 17 months?

In September 2026, the police reported that the Enugu Command detained 6,240 suspects, recovered 156 firearms and 1,486 rounds of ammunition, and rescued 211 kidnapped victims over the 17 months from 11 March 2025. These are police-reported figures for that period.

Which LGAs had the highest ACLED incident counts between January 2024 and August 2025?

Isi-Uzo had 24, Enugu North 18, Nsukka 14, Uzo-Uwani 13 and Udi 12 recorded incidents, according to the EUAA report based on ACLED data.

Does the state government control the police and military?

The police and military are federal institutions. The state government supports security through funding, logistics, infrastructure, technology, coordination and collaboration. Joint operations may involve state and federal actors.

Has every LGA received the same security equipment?

The public information describes statewide systems and specific LGA-level initiatives, but it does not provide a complete public inventory showing identical equipment or staffing in every LGA. AskEnugu should identify the interventions specifically reported for each area.

Are Forest Guards the same as the police?

No. Forest Guards are a state-supported security formation focused on forest and rural security. The Nigeria Police Force retains its policing and criminal investigation responsibilities.

Why are roads included in a security discussion?

Road access can help security teams reach isolated communities, support emergency response and improve movement around rural and farming areas. This is why some road projects in Isi-Uzo and other rural locations have been discussed alongside security measures.
