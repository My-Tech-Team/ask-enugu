ENUGU STATE ECONOMY AND TAX
A Detailed Record of Economic Development, Revenue Reforms and Business Support under the Governor Peter Ndubuisi Mbah Administration

Coverage: 29 May 2023 to 16 November 2026

1. Understanding Enugu’s economic responsibilities
Economic development in Enugu State involves several institutions working together. The Enugu State Government sets policy, prepares budgets, builds infrastructure, supports businesses and coordinates investment. The Enugu State Internal Revenue Service collects taxes and other government revenue. The Enugu SME Center supports micro, small and medium enterprises, startups and innovation.

The state government does not control every business, investor or federal agency. Therefore, when a project involves private companies, federal institutions or development partners, AskEnugu should explain the role of the state government as a promoter, partner, regulator or supporter where that is what the available report establishes.

2. Economic situation before May 2023
Before Governor Peter Mbah assumed office on 29 May 2023, Enugu State already faced economic pressures. These included limited industrial activity, dependence on public-sector spending, infrastructure gaps, unemployment, low private investment, weak revenue collection and the wider economic effects of insecurity in the South-East.

These problems did not begin with the Mbah administration. They formed part of the economic environment that the incoming government had to address.

The main challenges included improving access to finance for small businesses, creating jobs for young people, expanding digital skills, attracting investors, improving infrastructure, strengthening tax collection and making government services easier to access.

3. The economic approach under Governor Peter Mbah
The administration’s approach has several connected parts. The first is economic expansion through investment, industrialisation and private-sector participation. The second is digital development through technology hubs, innovation programmes and outsourcing. The third is revenue reform through tax administration and automation. The fourth is infrastructure support through roads, transport, power and public facilities.

The idea behind this approach is that economic growth requires more than government announcements. Businesses need electricity, roads, security, skilled workers, access to finance, clear rules and reliable public services.

4. Major economic initiatives
4.1 The $30 billion economy target
One of the administration’s central economic targets is to grow Enugu’s economy from about $4.4 billion to $30 billion by 2031. This is a long-term policy target, not a claim that the target has already been achieved.

The target is intended to guide investment, infrastructure, industrialisation and job creation. AskEnugu should always describe it as a target for 2031, not as a current achievement.

4.2 Enugu SME Center
The Enugu SME Center is the state institution responsible for supporting micro, small and medium enterprises, startups and innovation. Its work includes business development, entrepreneurship training, digital skills, market access and strategic partnerships.

The Center also supports innovation activities such as hackathons. One reported hackathon received more than 250 applications from six local institutions; 35 teams entered an innovation boot camp, and the top 10 teams presented their solutions publicly.

AskEnugu should describe the SME Center as a state-supported institution for business and innovation, not as a replacement for private investors, banks or federal business-support agencies.

4.3 Tech and Innovation Hub
The Enugu Tech and Innovation Hub is intended to support startups, innovators and technology entrepreneurs. It is part of the state’s effort to build a digital economy and create technology-based employment.

The hub is designed to bring together young people, developers, founders, investors and business-support organisations. AskEnugu should avoid claiming that every young person in Enugu has directly benefited unless a specific programme or beneficiary is documented.

4.4 Artificial Intelligence Institute
The Artificial Intelligence Institute is part of the administration’s digital-economy agenda. It is intended to develop digital skills and support AI-related solutions for business, public services and innovation.

The institute is important because artificial intelligence, data and digital skills are increasingly relevant to modern jobs and businesses. However, its impact depends on training, access to technology, internet connectivity and employment opportunities.

4.5 Enugu Talent City
Enugu Talent City is a large-scale knowledge and outsourcing ecosystem intended to create digital-economy employment. Public descriptions mention a 2,000-seat knowledge-based facility and a 750-seat Business Process Outsourcing centre.

The Talent City is intended to support young people seeking digital jobs, technology companies seeking talent, and businesses seeking outsourcing services. AskEnugu should distinguish between the project’s planned capacity and the number of people actually employed.

4.6 Industrial investment
The administration has also emphasised attracting industrial investment. Public reporting has referred to a $20 million technology investment connected to local manufacturing and the Enugu Haier Factory, described as a partnership involving the state government and Haier Group.

Industrial projects can support jobs, local production and economic diversification. However, their real impact depends on completion, employment levels, local sourcing, power supply, logistics and continued operation.

5. Tax and revenue reforms
5.1 Enugu State Internal Revenue Service
The Enugu State Internal Revenue Service, ESIRS, is the main state agency responsible for tax administration and revenue collection. Its work includes assessing taxes, collecting revenue, registering taxpayers, enforcing compliance and improving taxpayer services.

The administration has described reforms at ESIRS as part of a wider effort to improve revenue generation, reduce leakages and make tax collection more efficient.

5.2 Internally Generated Revenue
Internally Generated Revenue, or IGR, is money generated by the state through taxes, levies, fees, licences and other non-federal sources. It is different from federal allocations, which come from the Federation Account.

ESIRS reported that Enugu’s IGR rose from about ₦26.8 billion in 2022 to ₦37.4 billion in 2023, ₦180.5 billion in 2024, and ₦406.7 billion in 2025.

These figures are revenue-collection figures, not profit figures and not a measure of citizens’ income. They also do not automatically prove that every household or business experienced improved economic conditions.

5.3 Tax-net expansion
The administration says it expanded the tax net by bringing more taxpayers into the system. This means identifying individuals and businesses that were previously not captured in the tax system and bringing them into formal tax administration.

Tax-net expansion is different from increasing tax rates. Expanding the tax net can increase the number of people or businesses paying taxes, while increasing tax rates means raising the percentage or amount charged on existing taxpayers.

5.4 Automation and e-payment
The administration has reported automation of revenue processes and a reduction in cash-based collections. Payments are intended to go directly into government accounts through electronic payment systems.

Automation is intended to reduce leakages, improve record-keeping and make it easier for taxpayers to pay. It also helps government track revenue more accurately.

5.5 One-stop tax shop
In February 2025, Governor Mbah signed the Enugu State Internal Revenue Service (Establishment and Consolidation of Revenue Administration) Law, 2025. The law was reported as creating a one-stop shop for tax collection and administration, ending multiple taxation and making ESIRS more autonomous.

This reform is important because it aims to simplify tax administration, reduce overlapping collections and improve the business environment.
