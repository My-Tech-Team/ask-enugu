ENUGU STATE EDUCATION
A Detailed Record of Education Challenges, Government Actions and Initiatives
Governor Peter Ndubuisi Mbah Administration
Coverage: 29 May 2023 to 17 November 2026
Prepared as a conversational knowledge source for AskEnugu AI
How AskEnugu AI should use this document
This document records what the Mbah administration has built, funded, launched, recruited and paid for in Enugu State's education sector, and the education problems it set out to address. It is written so AskEnugu AI can answer natural questions by explaining actions and connecting them: what was done, where, when, who carried it out, and what it was meant to change.
A user may ask what the government built, how many teachers were recruited, what the Smart Green Schools include, or what support exists for university students. Answers should lead with the action and its date, then give the local detail.
The account does not claim every education problem has been solved. It also separates documented actions (contracts awarded, schools opened, teachers trained, budgets approved, arrears paid) from figures that come only from state records. Where a number comes from the state's own reporting and has not been independently audited, AskEnugu should state it with its date, for example "state records put the number at 267 in April 2026". Visual evidence supplied by the AskEnugu team (photographs, documents, videos) is marked below with [Evidence] lines, so the chatbot can point users to it.
1. Who delivers education in Enugu
Basic education (pre-primary to junior secondary) is managed by the Enugu State Universal Basic Education Board (ENSUBEB). Senior secondary schools are managed by the Post Primary Schools Management Board, and the new Science, Technical and Vocational (STV) schools by their own management board. The Ministry of Education, led by Commissioner Professor Ndubueze Mbah, coordinates policy. Scholarships are run by the Enugu State Scholarship and Education Loans Board (ESSELB). State-owned tertiary institutions have their own councils. Examinations (WAEC, NECO, JAMB) and UBEC are federal. AskEnugu should credit the right institution for each action.
2. The education situation the administration set out to change
Before and at the start of the administration, the state's own assessments identified several problems.
• Weak foundational learning. The Special Adviser on Education Innovation, Chinyere Onyeisi, reported that the state's assessment of public schools found about three in four children with foundational literacy problems, with pupils often promoted without mastering the work.
• Dropout. The Ministry of Education cites a 2019 UNESCO study that found a 48 percent dropout rate. Separately, 2023 official statistics show Enugu with 89.46 percent literacy and 7.5 percent out-of-school children, among the lowest in Nigeria. AskEnugu should give both and explain that the state's position is that high enrolment hid weak learning.
• Ageing buildings and equipment. Many public school buildings date from the colonial era, with shortages of classrooms, equipment and teachers. The Nigeria Union of Teachers raised teacher recruitment and unpaid promotions in October 2023.
• Unpaid tertiary staff. At the Enugu State College of Education (Technical), staff were owed eight months of salary from the previous administration.
3. The approach
The administration's education programme has five connected parts: funding (about one third of the budget), new school buildings (Smart Green Schools, senior secondary schools, STV schools), a change in teaching method from rote learning to experiential learning, teacher recruitment and training, and access support (free uniforms, books, meals, scholarships). The Centre for Experiential Learning and Innovation (CELI), whose pilot is being built at the College of Education (Technical), leads curriculum, teacher training and research.
4. Funding for education
The Mbah administration has allocated about one third of the state budget to education every year. The 2024 budget gave education 33 percent. The 2026 Appropriation Bill, presented on 2 December 2025, totalled ₦1.62 trillion and gave education about ₦521 billion, the largest sector share (about 32 percent). Items inside the 2026 education budget include ₦158 billion for 16 smart senior secondary schools, ₦30 billion for school meals for about 260,000 pupils each school day, and ₦68 billion for technical and vocational schools (reports differ on whether this covers 10 or 110 schools).
These are budget allocations. AskEnugu should not describe them as money already spent, and should say no audited education expenditure figure has been published in the sources reviewed.

5. Enugu Smart Green Schools
5.1 What the administration built
The Smart Green School programme is the centrepiece of the education agenda. The administration built Smart Green School campuses across the state's 260 political wards, covering pre-primary to junior secondary. The number built stands at 268 as of October 2026, because some wards have more than one campus. In many places one new campus replaces several smaller older schools. Contracts for 127 campuses were awarded in 2023 and a further 133 in 2024.
Each campus is built with about 25 digitally connected classrooms, ICT centres, robotics and AI labs, an e-library, solar power, internet connectivity, a health clinic, water storage, a smart farm for practical agriculture and housing for teachers on site. Free uniforms, books and meals are part of the model.
[Evidence: photographs of completed campuses, classrooms, labs, smart farms, teacher housing]
5.2 Timeline
• November 2023. Construction began on the programme of over 250 schools across the wards. Contracts for 127 campuses were awarded in 2023 and 133 more in 2024.
• January 2024. The pilot campus at Owo, Nkanu East, opened with 865 pupils. By October 2024, 60 of its first junior secondary graduates had gained admission with full scholarships to Holy Rosary College and the College of Immaculate Conception in Enugu.
• August 2024. The government set its siting criteria: campuses must be central and close to residential areas, and projects do not start in communities that fail to meet them.
• August 2025. The Akpawfu campus in Nkanu East was inaugurated, among others.
• 22 September 2025. The governor inaugurated the Smart Green Schools for the 2025/2026 session, and basic education transitioned into them. Some campuses opened later than others because of construction delays.
• November 2026. The governor and UBEC's executive secretary commissioned further campuses, including Ibagwa Nike, Ugbo Odogwu, Airport and Amankpaka Nike in Enugu East, and campuses in Aninri and at Uhueze Nenwe.
[Evidence: Owo pilot opening photos, handover or commissioning photos by LGA]
5.3 Quality control
The government has acted on construction problems. It demolished a Smart Green School at Mbu, Isi-Uzo, over substandard work and structural defects, and terminated a contract over defects at another site, saying its own monitoring had flagged the problem before a video circulated online. In October 2026 the governor acknowledged structural defects in some campuses and warned contractors that they would be blacklisted if they did not fix them. These actions show an enforcement mechanism, but also that some construction fell short of the standard set.
5.4 Independent recognition
UBEC, UNICEF and UNESCO have publicly commended the programme. UNICEF said it supported the Enugu State Education Sector Plan 2026–2035. TechCabal's April 2026 reporting described pilot pupils doing data collection on smart farms and building simple robots.
5.5 Challenges that remain
AskEnugu should be open that the programme is still being completed and that concerns have been raised.
• Distance. A report in April 2026 said some pupils live up to about ten kilometres from their new Smart Green School and warned of dropout risk. The sources reviewed contain no official dropout figure confirming or rejecting this.
• Unfinished facilities. The governor acknowledged setbacks and delays. On 20 October 2026, SaharaReporters reported a flooded compound, unfinished structures and poor sanitation at the Emene school. No official response was found in the sources reviewed.
• Cost and contracts. A 2025 public controversy concerned a contractor, Sujimoto Luxury Construction Limited, and questions about upfront payment and price per school. A citizens' group called for an audit in September 2025.
6. Teachers
Recruitment. The state ran an open screening for about 6,000 Smart Green School teaching posts; more than 26,000 people applied. On 29 July 2026, ENSUBEB announced approval to recruit over 6,500 qualified candidates into academic, technical and administrative posts in public primary and secondary schools, including the Smart Green Schools (BusinessDay reported 6,580 vacancies). Different announcements cover different rounds, so AskEnugu should not add them together.
Training. In 2025 the state completed phase one of digital and modern-method training for more than 18,000 public school teachers, new and existing, under the Teachers Professional Development Policy. By October 2026 the governor put the number of teachers trained in digital literacy above 23,000. From 1 August to 12 September 2025, about 1,000 science, technical and vocational teachers were trained with ₦30 million approved for the course.
Pay. The state set up a committee in October 2023 on the ₦30,000 minimum wage for primary school teachers. By March 2024 the NUT state chairman confirmed wage awards had been paid on top of salaries since December 2023.
[Evidence: training session photos, recruitment notices, appointment lists, NUT statement]
7. Secondary, technical and vocational schools
The administration rebuilt Government Technical College (GTC), Enugu as the first Science, Technical and Vocational (STV) school. The First Lady of Nigeria, Senator Oluremi Tinubu, commissioned it on 9 June 2025 as the pilot for technical and vocational education in the state. The college now runs a digital and industrial training programme with the company Haier for senior secondary students. Seven more STV schools are planned, one for each of the remaining federal constituencies. The three models are science and technology, building and architecture, and industrial agriculture. Smart Green School graduates are meant to move on to 17 smart senior secondary schools, one per LGA, or to STV schools. On 28 September 2024 the government closed schools it found illegal or substandard.
The seven remaining STV schools and the 17 smart senior secondary schools have not been reported complete in the sources reviewed.
[Evidence: GTC Enugu site photos, STV school progress photos, closure enforcement notices]
8. Tertiary education
• ESUT. The university secured full accreditation in law, engineering and sciences in May 2025, and the governor reported 97 percent of its programmes accredited. In August 2026, amid an ASUU strike, the monthly subvention was raised by ₦115 million, from ₦161 million to ₦276 million. Whether the strike has ended is not confirmed.
• Arrears. The administration approved payment of about ₦467 million in eight-month salary arrears owed to staff of the Enugu State College of Education (Technical).
• Policy. All state-owned tertiary institutions are required to deliver experiential learning. The state plans an Enugu State University of Education and Technology and a University of Agriculture and Technology.
• Scholarships. The 2025 scheme offered 1,600 slots: 500 undergraduates, 100 postgraduates and 1,000 teachers' master's places (teachers commit to ten years' service). In June 2026 the board said over 760 beneficiaries were approved for 2025/2026.
[Evidence: accreditation letters, subvention approval, arrears payment document, scholarship list]
9. Policy framework
On 7 October 2026, at the Enugu Education Summit, the administration unveiled the Enugu State Education Policy, which guides the sector for six to ten years, alongside the Education Sector Plan 2026–2035.
10. Education interventions across the 17 LGAs
The Smart Green School programme covers every ward in the state, so every LGA is part of it. On 4 January 2025, President Bola Tinubu commissioned the first 30 completed and equipped Smart Green Schools in Enugu. The entries below record the specific, documented activity for each LGA. In November 2026 the governor began his tour of development centres in Aninri West, where he commissioned the Amorji, Uhueze and Agbada campuses of the Smart Green School at Nenwe and met pupils there.
